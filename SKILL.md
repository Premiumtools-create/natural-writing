# Natural Writing

## Understand the request

Identify the audience, purpose, genre, tone, word limit and required format.

Distinguish between exact transcription, punctuation-only editing,
natural rewriting and fresh drafting.

During an ongoing humanising task, rewrite text supplied in an image
unless the user asks for an exact transcription. Work only with readable
text. Do not invent content missing from a cropped image.

Use a supplied writing sample to understand the user's vocabulary,
formality, contractions, sentence rhythm and paragraph habits.
Do not copy unrelated facts from the sample.

Default to Australian spelling unless instructed otherwise.
Ask for the text if none is available.

## Preserve meaning

Identify and preserve:
- Every distinct substantive point.
- Names, dates, numbers and personal details.
- Technical terms, quotations and citations.
- Qualifications, uncertainty and attribution.
- The difference between association and causation.

Do not invent facts, sources, memories, feelings, opinions or anecdotes
to make the writing sound personal.

Do not change “may help” into “will help”.
Do not change “is associated with” into “causes”.
Preserve quotations exactly unless editing them is explicitly authorised.

## Improve the writing

Replace empty openings with the actual subject.
Remove filler, repeated points and unnecessary announcements.
Prefer clear verbs over inflated phrases.
Replace vague praise with precise details supported by the source.
Connect ideas according to their meaning.
Use transitions only where they help the reader follow the argument.
Remove conclusions that merely repeat earlier sentences unless a
formal conclusion is required.

Improve awkward sentence structure rather than mechanically swapping
words for synonyms.

Vary sentence structure where it improves readability.
Do not impose a fixed sentence-length pattern.
Keep passages that already work.
Avoid forced slang, deliberate errors, random fragments and obscure
synonyms.

Treat writing patterns as editing cues, not proof of AI authorship.
Do not use a blanket list of forbidden words.

## Adapt to the genre

Academic:
Use a formal register, precise terminology, citations and appropriate
caution. Avoid unsupported claims and invented personal reflections.

Personal:
Use a conversational tone appropriate to the user's sample.
Retain the experiences provided without adding fictional details.

Professional:
State the request, decision or main point early.
Include the details the reader needs to act.

School essays:
Use accessible language and retain the expected essay structure.
Do not make the writer sound artificially childish.

## Simplify punctuation

Remove decorative symbols and genuinely unnecessary punctuation.

Retain punctuation needed for grammar, readability or meaning,
including essential commas, apostrophes, hyphens, decimal points,
mathematical symbols, quotation marks and citation punctuation.

For punctuation-only requests, preserve all words and their order.

Example:
“I enjoy reading!!! I also like, playing cricket.”
becomes:
“I enjoy reading. I also like playing cricket.”

Keep:
“Let's eat, Grandma.”
The comma changes the meaning and must remain.

## Review and correct

After drafting:

1. Compare the revision with the source for omissions, inventions,
changed meaning, altered certainty, numbers and attribution.
2. Check for awkward phrasing, repetition, filler, forced rhythm
and inconsistent tone.
3. Check grammar, spelling, punctuation, citations, format and length.
Count words when the limit is strict.
4. Correct identified problems and recheck affected passages.

Repeat until the requirements pass.
Do not keep rewriting passages without a concrete reason.
If a material ambiguity remains, identify it or ask a focused question.

## Deliver

Return the revised text directly in the requested format.
Include an editing explanation only when requested.
Do not add promotional claims or unnecessary commentary.

Never invent detector results or claim a guaranteed percentage of
human authorship. AI-assisted editing cannot guarantee a detector score.
Distinguish editorial checks from actual external detector tests.
Do not upload user text to an external detector without permission.
Respect authorship and disclosure requirements supplied by the user.
